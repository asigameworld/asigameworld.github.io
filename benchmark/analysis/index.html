Self-management

As models adapt to the game environment and continuously revise their scripts to play, they also need to keep their character's condition in check. Each column represents one hour. The stacked bar shows how actions were split across building, combat, crafting, and exploration, while the curves plot hourly averages for health, fullness, and stamina. When a curve drops while the bar stays active, it signals state mismanagement, as the script kept pushing tasks without looking after itself.

Pick a model, then hover or tap any hour to inspect exact physiology averages and task counts.

Action library rejection & error recovery

When agents call the in-game action library, calls that violate game rules—such as missing materials, out of range, blocked siting, or action cooldowns—are rejected by the engine. Note that a rising rejection rate in later hours often reflects task escalation rather than model degradation: agents transition from simple early tasks (foraging, starter quests, basic tools) to harder mid/late-game tasks (house siting & construction, multi-floor dungeon boss combat, and high-tier crafting). Stronger models briefly spike when entering a harder domain and then rapidly adapt to drive rejections back down.
